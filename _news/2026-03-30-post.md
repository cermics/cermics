 The <a href="http://smai.emath.fr/spip.php?article165&amp;lang=fr" target="_blank">SMAI-GAMNI Ph.D. prize</a> was created in 2005 to reward doctoral research in applied mathematics for engineering sciences. 
 
Each year, the prize is awarded to a thesis defended in France within a range of topics, including PDEs, numerical analysis, scientific computing, mechanics, and climate modeling. The laureate is then nominated by SMAI-GAMNI as a candidate for the European Ph.D. awards of the <a href="https://eccomas.org/awards/phd-awards/" target="_blank">ECCOMAS society</a>. 
  
This year, <a href="http://smai.emath.fr/spip.php?article977&amp;lang=fr" target="_blank">the prize was awarded</a> to <a href="https://noeblassel.github.io" target="_blank">Noé Blassel</a> for <a href="https://theses.hal.science/tel-05489722" target="_blank">his thesis</a> on the mathematical analysis and computation of trajectorial properties in molecular dynamics. Defended in December 2025, the thesis was carried out at CERMICS under the supervision of <a href="https://cermics-lab.enpc.fr/tony-lelievre/" target="_blank">Tony Lelièvre</a> and <a href="https://cermics-lab.enpc.fr/gabriel-stoltz/" target="_blank">Gabriel Stoltz</a>. 
   
You can read <a href="https://ingenius.ecoledesponts.fr/articles/observer-linvisible-simuler-le-mouvement-des-atomes-grace-aux-maths/" target="_blank">his interview</a> about his prize.
